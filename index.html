🏢 Service Companies

What they do: Build software for other companies

Examples: TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Deloitte

Great for: Exposure to diverse projects and tech stacks

Technology stack is often determined by client requirements

Great for: Deep expertise and cutting-edge technology

Use the latest technologies to stay competitive

Career Strategy: Start in service companies to learn broadly across industries and technologies. Then transition to product companies for deeper specialization and higher compensation.

05 — Tech Stack

Choose Wisely

A 6-point framework to pick the right technology stack based on your profile.

Interest-Based

What excites you?
  • Visual apps → JavaScript
  • System logic → Java/Python
  • Data & predictions → Python/R
  • Mobile apps → Kotlin/Swift

Market Demand

Where are the jobs?
  • Mass hiring: Java, Python, JS
  • High growth: Python (AI/ML), React
  • High-pay niche: Cyber, iOS
  • Always needed: SQL/Databases

Future Scope

Long-term growth
  • Java: 25+ years dominant
  • Python: AI/ML revolution
  • JavaScript: Web is everywhere
  • Cloud: Every company migrating

Learning Curve

How fast to start?
  • Easiest: HTML/CSS → Quick start
  • Beginner: Python → Fast results
  • Moderate: Java/JS → Steady pace
  • Advanced: AI/ML → Long journey

Background-Based

Your starting point
  • Complete beginner → Python
  • C/C++ background → Java
  • Math strong → Data Science
  • Design skills → UI/UX

Career Growth

Professional trajectory
  • Web: Thousands of openings
  • Mobile: Specialized, high-value
  • Data/AI: Highest growth field
  • Cloud: Future-proof for decades

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗

Trend Chasing

Don't learn AI just because everyone else is doing it. Find genuine interest first.

✗

Multiple Languages at Once

Master ONE language before adding another. Breadth too early = shallow knowledge.

✗

Following Friends Blindly

Your career path must match your strengths and interests, not your peers.

✗

Switching Too Often

Commit to one stack for at least 6 months. Consistency builds real skill.

08 — Resume

ATS-Ready Resume

Recruiters spend just 6 seconds on your resume. Make every second count.

✓ DO

  • Use standard fonts
  • Simple clean formatting
  • Export as PDF
  • Include keywords from JD
  • Keep it one page

✗ DON'T

  • Use graphics or images
  • Use tables or columns
  • Use fancy templates
  • Write more than one page
  • Leave spelling errors

The 6-Second Scan Rule

0-2 sec

Name & Education

2-4 sec

Skills Match Check

4-6 sec

Projects & Proof

Resume Structure

  • 01 Contact Information (top center)
  • 02 Education (degree, college, CGPA)
  • 03 Technical & Soft Skills
  • 04 Projects (most critical section!)
  • 05 Internships / Training (if any)

10 — Interviews

Crack Every Interview

Know the structure, prepare for the patterns, and practice until it's natural.

R1

Online Test

Aptitude + Coding + MCQs

⏱ 45-90 minutes
R2

Technical Interview 1

Core concepts + 1-2 coding problems

⏱ 30-60 minutes
R3

Technical Interview 2

Projects discussion + problem solving

⏱ 30-45 minutes
HR

HR Round

Culture fit + salary + joining details

⏱ 15-30 minutes

Technical Questions

  • Explain OOP concepts with real examples
  • Difference between Array and ArrayList
  • What is polymorphism? Real-world example?
  • Explain your best project in detail
  • Write code to reverse a string
  • Find duplicate elements in an array
  • What is REST API? How does it work?
  • Explain database normalization (1NF–3NF)

HR Questions

  • Tell me about yourself (2 min max)
  • Why should we hire you over others?
  • What are your key strengths and weaknesses?
  • Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
  • Why do you want to join our company specifically?
  • Tell me about a challenge you overcame
  • Are you comfortable relocating?
  • What is your expected salary range?
